📊 Financial Metrics

Hover any metric for definition & formula

Net Service Revenue (NSR)

Total revenue from services delivered, excluding pass-through costs. The primary top-line measure for a services firm.

NSR = Total Invoiced

NSR

Net Service Revenue

Gross Service Revenue (GSR)

Total billings including pass-through costs (hardware, licences, travel) recharged to clients. Larger than NSR.

GSR = NSR + Pass-throughs

GSR

Gross Service Revenue

Effective Service Revenue (ESR)

NSR after deducting subcontractor costs. Represents revenue earned by your own staff — the base for margin calculations.

ESR = NSR - Subcontractor Costs

ESR

Effective Service Revenue

Gross Margin %

Percentage of NSR remaining after direct delivery costs (timesheet burn). Shows how efficiently you deliver services.

(NSR - Delivery Cost) / NSR

Contribution Margin %

ESR remaining after all variable costs (delivery + subcontractors). Shows how much each dollar of own-staff revenue contributes to covering fixed costs.

(ESR - Delivery Cost) / ESR

EBITDA

Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation & Amortisation. Proxy for operating cash generation. Enter overhead above to activate.

EBITDA Margin %

EBITDA as a percentage of ESR. Industry benchmark for IT services firms is 15–25%. Below 10% signals cost pressure.

EBITDA / ESR × 100

Revenue per Head

NSR divided by total headcount. Key productivity metric — IT services firms typically target $150k–$300k per head annually.

NSR / Headcount

Utilisation Rate

Billable hours logged as a % of total available hours (headcount × hrs/wk × 4 weeks). Target 75–85% for healthy delivery teams.

Billable Hrs / (Heads × Hrs/Wk × 4)

Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

Average days to collect payment. Lower is better — above 60 days signals cash flow risk. Target under 45 days.

(Outstanding / NSR) × 30

Win Rate

Percentage of closed opportunities that were won. IT services benchmark is 25–40%. Low win rate signals pricing or proposal quality issues.
